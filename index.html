04 / A RELATED BEHAVIOUR

E-cigarette use across generations

Vaping is a separate measure from smoking. These charts provide additional context on how current e-cigarette use varies by age and survey period.

7. E-cigarette use across age groups

E-cigarette use has changed across Australian age groups over successive National Drug Strategy Household Survey periods. This heat map compares the percentage of people currently using e-cigarettes from 2016 to 2025, highlighting differences between younger and older age groups.

Loading Chart 7...
How to read this chart: Each cell represents an age group in a particular survey year. Darker green indicates a higher reported percentage currently using e-cigarettes. Hover over a cell to inspect its estimate and source flag. Estimates marked with an asterisk should be used with caution.

Source: Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), National Drug Strategy Household Survey 2025, Table 2.5: Use of e-cigarettes, by age and gender, 2016 to 2025. Estimates marked with an asterisk should be used with caution. The # symbol identifies a statistically significant change between 2022–2023 and 2025, according to the source table.

8. How smoking prevalence has changed across generations

Comparing daily smoking prevalence in 2001 and 2025 highlights how patterns differ across adult age groups. The slope chart connects the two survey years to show whether prevalence has increased or decreased, with separate panels for males and females.

Loading Chart 8...
How to read this chart: Each line represents an age group. A downward slope indicates lower daily smoking prevalence in 2025 than in 2001, while an upward slope indicates a higher estimate. Compare the size and direction of the changes between age groups and between males and females. Hover over a point to inspect its estimate and source flag.

Source: Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), National Drug Strategy Household Survey 2025, Table 1.4: Daily smoking by age and sex, 2001–2025. Estimates with source flags should be interpreted according to the original table's notes.

9. How smoking prevalence reshapes the map

This tile-style cartogram represents Australian states and territories as squares whose areas are proportional to their age-standardised daily smoking prevalence in 2022. Unlike a conventional map, the sizes of the squares change to emphasise differences in the reported prevalence estimates.

Loading Chart 9...
How to read this cartogram: Each square represents a state or territory. Larger square areas indicate higher age-standardised daily smoking prevalence, while smaller areas indicate lower prevalence. The percentage is printed inside each square, and hovering reveals the jurisdiction and estimate. The layout is schematic rather than a geographically exact map.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), National Health Survey 2022, age-standardised daily smoking estimates for states and territories. Values are expressed as percentages.

10. How vaping estimates changed between 2022–2023 and 2025

This dumbbell chart compares current e-cigarette use across Australian age groups between the 2022–2023 and 2025 survey periods. The connecting lines highlight the direction and size of the difference between the two estimates.

Loading Chart 10...
How to read this chart: Each row represents an age group. Green shows the 2022–2023 estimate, while orange shows the 2025 estimate. A dot further to the right indicates a higher percentage. The largest visible gap is among 18–24-year-olds, whose estimate fell from 20.6% to 14.0%. These are survey estimates, so differences should be interpreted in the context of the source table's notes.

Source: Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW), National Drug Strategy Household Survey 2025, Table 2.5: Use of e-cigarettes, by age and gender, 2016 to 2025. Estimates marked with source flags should be interpreted according to the original table's notes.

Loading Chart 11...

Reading the data responsibly

Smoking and e-cigarette use are distinct measures, and estimates from different surveys may use different age ranges or definitions. Check each chart’s subtitle, source note and tooltip before comparing values. Where source flags or margins of error are supplied, interpret estimates using the original table notes.
